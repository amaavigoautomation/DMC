## UAE case study draft

### Connecting individual bookings with shared service operations

**Illustrative scenario:** A UAE inbound DMC handles hotel stays, airport transfers, city tours, desert safaris and attraction bookings for overseas agents and direct travellers.

A booking may look straightforward to sales, yet require several operational decisions. Guests arrive on different flights, stay in different hotels and join shared services supplied by different partners. When booking details, supplier confirmations and pickup arrangements sit in separate spreadsheets and messages, operations must repeatedly reconstruct what was sold.

**How DMC24 would support the workflow**

- Record the enquiry, customer requirements and booking services in a connected file.

- Prepare quotations using contracted products and applicable selling rates.

- Manage individual service bookings where included in the selected edition.

- Organise SIC transfers and SIC tours, with clubbing of eligible safari and park services.

- Coordinate supplier reservations, vouchers and operational assignments.

- Track customer receivables, supplier payables and file profitability.

**A typical working day**

Several agents confirm guests for the same safari departure. Each customer booking retains its commercial details, while operations identifies services that can be clubbed and coordinates the shared delivery. Finance can review the related income and costs without rebuilding the trip from separate records.

**Business value to demonstrate**

The demo should show how a booking moves from sales to execution, how shared services remain traceable to individual files, and how teams review outstanding confirmations and margins.

**Measures for a real client study:** quotation turnaround, repeated data entry, missed pickup or service changes, and time to review file profitability.

**Call to action:** See how DMC24 connects your UAE bookings, SIC services and operational finance.

## Sri Lanka case study draft

### Keeping a touring itinerary commercially and operationally connected

**Illustrative scenario:** A Sri Lanka DMC designs private and group itineraries combining cultural visits, hill-country stays, wildlife experiences and beach extensions.

The sales team must balance hotel choices, vehicle requirements, route distances, entrance fees and market-specific rates. A change to one overnight stop may affect several other services. If the quotation and operating file are maintained separately, staff must check each version manually before confirming suppliers.

**How DMC24 would support the workflow**

- Maintain hotel and service contracts for different markets and seasons.

- Build FIT and group quotations from the relevant products and rates.

- Account for transport, mileage and applicable entrance-fee categories.

- Convert an accepted quotation into a booking for supplier coordination.

- Produce guest and supplier documents from the booking information.

- Track operating expenses, receivables, payables and file-wise profitability.

**A typical enquiry**

An overseas agent requests a cultural and hill-country circuit followed by a beach stay. The client then asks for a different hotel category and an additional night. The team revises the itinerary and costing in the connected workflow, confirms the selected version and carries those details into reservations and operations.

**Business value to demonstrate**

The central benefit is continuity: sales, reservations, operations and finance work from the same commercial plan. The demonstration should make clear how itinerary changes affect services and costs before the team commits to delivery.

**Measures for a real client study:** time to issue a revised quotation, supplier confirmation follow-ups, uncosted itinerary changes and time to close a completed file.

**Call to action:** Explore a complete Sri Lanka enquiry-to-operation workflow with DMC24.

## Maldives case study draft

### Bringing resort offers and island transfers into one quotation workflow

**Illustrative scenario:** A Maldives specialist sells resort holidays through international agents, with different room categories, meal plans, guest compositions and transfer arrangements.

The challenge is often in the conditions behind a rate. A resort offer may depend on booking dates, travel dates, length of stay or permitted combinations. Transfer costs and arrangements also need to match the chosen resort and guest booking. Separate rate sheets make these checks difficult to repeat consistently.

**How DMC24 would support the workflow**

- Maintain resort contracts, room categories, meal plans and relevant guest policies.

- Manage supported tactical and combined offers according to configured rules.

- Include speedboat, seaplane or domestic-flight transfer services in the booking.

- Record applicable tax settings and commercial terms.

- Prepare quotations for sharing through email and WhatsApp.

- Coordinate reservations, supplier documents, customer invoicing and financial reporting.

**A typical enquiry**

An agent requests two resort options for a family holiday. The reservations team compares the applicable contracted rates, offer conditions, room arrangements and transfers. Once the agent selects an option, the team progresses the same booking details through supplier confirmation and payment tracking.

**Business value to demonstrate**

The demo should show the reasoning behind the quotation: which resort rate applies, which offers can be used, which transfer is included and what remains to be confirmed. This makes the commercial basis easier for both sales and finance to review.

**Measures for a real client study:** rate-checking time, quotation corrections, offer-condition errors and unresolved transfer confirmations.

**Call to action:** See how DMC24 supports Maldives resort sales, offers and transfer coordination.

## Mauritius case study draft

### Connecting contracted offers with sales after arrival

**Illustrative scenario:** A Mauritius DMC serves overseas travel partners and also sells excursions and other services through destination representatives.

A French agent requests a customised holiday with a hotel stay, airport transfers and selected activities. Before confirmation, the sales team must evaluate the applicable hotel contract and offers. After arrival, the guests may purchase additional experiences through a representative. Those later sales need a clear relationship to booking, payment and commission records.

**How DMC24 would support the workflow**

- Maintain hotel contracts and market-specific selling arrangements.

- Apply supported single, combined, tactical and cross-season offer rules.

- Manage blackout periods, stop-sale and free-sale settings, and allotment or allocation information.

- Prepare quotations and coordinate hotel, transfer and activity reservations.

- Use the optional Local Sales extension for representative-led service sales.

- Review receipts, supplier costs, commissions and booking profitability.

**A typical guest journey**

The pre-arrival team confirms the holiday requested by the agent. During the stay, a representative sells an additional excursion through Local Sales. The sales transaction, collection and commission can then be reviewed alongside the relevant operational and financial records, according to the configured workflow.

**Business value to demonstrate**

The opportunity is to connect two parts of the business: winning the original booking and managing additional destination sales. Both need consistent products, prices, fulfilment and financial control.

**Measures for a real client study:** offer-checking time, additional sales per guest, collection reconciliation time and commission adjustments.

**Call to action:** Discover how DMC24 can connect your Mauritius contracting, bookings and Local Sales.

## India Kerala case study draft

### Managing a customised Kerala journey from quotation to final accounts

**Illustrative scenario:** A Kerala inbound operator assembles private journeys combining city stays, hill stations, wildlife visits, houseboat experiences and beach stays.

Each itinerary draws on several suppliers. Travellers may change hotel categories, transfer arrangements or the length of their stay before confirming. Once travel starts, operations needs the final service plan, while finance needs visibility into advances, balances and supplier commitments.

**How DMC24 would support the workflow**

- Maintain contracted hotels, transport, activities and other applicable services.

- Build a customised quotation with a clear service and cost breakdown.

- Revise itinerary options before converting the chosen proposal into a booking.

- Coordinate supplier reservations and issue the relevant vouchers.

- Track customer collections, supplier payments and operational expenses.

- Review the completed booking’s income, costs and profitability.

**A typical enquiry**

A family requests a Kerala holiday combining Kochi, Munnar, a houseboat stay and a beach extension. Sales presents suitable options, revises the selected programme and confirms the booking. Operations then uses the agreed service details to coordinate suppliers, while finance monitors deposits, balances and costs against the same file.

**Business value to demonstrate**

The focus is on managing change without losing the commercial picture. A useful demo would follow one booking through quotation revisions, supplier confirmation, travel delivery and financial closure.

**Measures for a real client study:** time to prepare itinerary alternatives, booking amendments requiring re-entry, unresolved supplier payments and time to finalise trip profitability.

**Call to action:** See how DMC24 can organise your Kerala touring business from the first enquiry to final accounts.

## Kenya case study draft

### Keeping safari quotations and field expenditure under control

**Illustrative scenario:** A Kenya safari DMC manages private and group itineraries involving camps, lodges, park visits, vehicles, driver-guides and operating cash.

A safari quotation depends on more than accommodation. Guest nationality, park fees, language-guide requirements and transport arrangements can affect both costing and delivery. During the trip, cash handed to the operating team must remain traceable. After travel, management needs to compare the planned margin with actual expenditure.

**How DMC24 would support the workflow**

- Maintain supplier contracts, direct offers and circuit offers.

- Prepare FIT, group and multi-nationality quotations, with charter-package quotations where applicable.

- Forecast park fees and manage language-guide selection.

- Coordinate transport requirements and the configured U-Turn Management workflow.

- Manage imprest and cash handover for field operations.

- Track supplier tax classifications and withholding settings where included.

- Review receivables, payables, file-wise profit and management reports.

**A typical safari file**

An agent requests a safari for travellers of different nationalities. The team prepares the applicable quotation, confirms camps and transport, records the guide requirement and plans operating cash. During financial closure, recorded expenses and supplier commitments provide the basis for reviewing actual profitability.

**Business value to demonstrate**

The demo should connect three views of the same safari: what was quoted, what operations must deliver and what the trip actually costs. This helps management investigate differences instead of looking only at a final revenue total.

**Measures for a real client study:** quotation revisions, park-fee adjustments, unsettled imprest, missing expense records and time to close safari files.

**Call to action:** Explore how DMC24 connects Kenya safari costing, operations and financial control.
