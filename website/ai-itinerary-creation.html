AI Itinerary Creation: Faster Proposals Without Losing Your Destination Expertise

Use AI to support itinerary writing while your team controls the accuracy and practicality of the journey.

A well-presented itinerary helps an agent or traveller imagine the experience before departure.

It explains how the journey unfolds, what each day includes and why the programme is worth choosing.

For a DMC consultant, producing that document can take considerable time. Even after the services have been costed, descriptions need writing, daily arrangements need explaining and the proposal needs presenting consistently.

AI assistance can support this work. The strongest results come when it works from a sound operational plan and remains subject to a knowledgeable team’s review.

Start with a Feasible Programme

Before writing begins, the itinerary needs a reliable foundation:

  • Confirmed travel dates and overnight locations.
  • Appropriate hotel and room arrangements.
  • Practical transfers and touring sequences.
  • Suitable service durations.
  • Clear inclusions and exclusions.
  • Relevant passenger requirements.

AI-generated wording should describe that programme accurately. Attractive language cannot resolve an impractical route or an unconfirmed service.

Your team’s destination knowledge remains central to deciding what the guest can realistically experience.

Use AI Where It Supports the Writing Process

AI can be useful for preparing a first draft, improving the flow of daily descriptions and making the tone more consistent across a proposal.

It can also help a consultant turn brief operational notes into clearer customer-facing explanations.

For example, an internal note might read:

“Arrival transfer, hotel check-in, evening free.”

A customer-facing version can explain the arrival sequence more naturally. However, it should not add a meet-and-greet service, guaranteed early check-in or an evening activity unless those arrangements are actually included.

The improvement should be in presentation, with the commercial scope kept accurate.

Establish a Human Review Before Sending

Every proposal needs a final review by someone who understands the destination and booking requirements.

Check that:

  • Place names and descriptions are accurate.
  • The programme follows a sensible geographical sequence.
  • Journey times and daily activity levels are realistic.
  • Hotel names and room categories match the quotation.
  • Included services are clearly distinguished from optional suggestions.
  • Meals, entrance fees and guiding arrangements are correctly described.
  • The wording makes no unsupported promises.

Information such as opening hours or access restrictions should be checked against current operational sources when relevant to the journey.

Keep the Proposal Connected to the Costing

An itinerary and a quotation should describe the same trip.

If a consultant updates the hotel, adds a service or changes the route, the customer-facing document needs to reflect that revision.

Otherwise, the DMC risks presenting an experience that differs from what was priced or booked.

A useful discipline is to review the itinerary against the accepted service list before it is sent, and again after material amendments.

Version control matters just as much when AI assists with writing.

Protect the DMC’s Own Voice

Your destination expertise is part of what agents buy from you.

A proposal should reflect your knowledge of local experiences, pacing and guest expectations. Generic descriptions can make distinctive programmes sound interchangeable.

Use AI drafts as material to refine. Add the details your team can confidently deliver: the reason for a particular route, the suitability of an experience or the advantage of an overnight location.

Those specific insights give the itinerary commercial value.
