From Enquiry to Profitability: Designing a Connected DMC Workflow

How sales, contracting, reservations, operations and finance can work around the same travel file.

A DMC can have capable people in every department and still lose time at the handovers between them.

Sales may consider a booking complete when the agent accepts. Reservations may still be waiting for a hotel. Operations may have a passenger list that no longer matches the latest amendment. Finance may be waiting for expenses before it can review the result.

A connected workflow makes the state of the booking understandable across the business. It defines what moves forward, who takes responsibility and what must be resolved before the next step.

Create a shared starting point

The enquiry record should establish the customer or agent, travel requirements and commercial context. The quotation then develops that information into services, costs and selling values.

Where several options are proposed, the accepted version needs to be clearly identifiable. Reservations should not have to interpret a chain of emails to work out which hotel or programme was selected.

The aim is to preserve the decisions already made while leaving unresolved items visible.

Define the handover conditions

Each department needs a practical definition of a complete handover. Sales may need to provide the accepted itinerary, passenger requirements and agreed payment terms. Reservations may need to provide service status, supplier references and outstanding conditions.

Those conditions should reflect how the DMC operates. A complicated approval process that staff routinely bypass will weaken the workflow rather than improve it.

Start with the information required to prevent common mistakes, and make responsibility for missing details clear.

Keep exceptions visible

A connected workflow does not mean every booking follows an identical path. Suppliers can decline requests, guests can change plans and costs can move after confirmation.

The process needs a way to identify the exception, assign an owner and record the resulting decision. A pending item should remain visible until it is resolved, rather than disappear inside an individual conversation.

When an amendment affects cost, service delivery or customer communication, the relevant departments need to receive that updated context.

Bring finance into the operating process

Financial visibility improves when cost and selling decisions remain connected to the booking throughout its life.

Supplier commitments, additional expenses, collections and amendments all contribute to the final result. Leaving them until file closure can make reconciliation more difficult and delay management insight.

Finance should help define which records are needed during operation so that the final review follows an established trail.

Agree what a closed file means

A completed trip is not necessarily a financially closed file. Supplier charges may still need checking, an expense may be missing or a customer balance may remain outstanding.

Define the checks required for operational completion and financial review separately. This gives management a clearer view of completed travel, pending obligations and files ready for profitability analysis.

Build the workflow before expanding it

DMC24 provides a connected mid-office foundation across these departments. Implementation should begin with representative bookings, agreed responsibilities and a manageable set of exceptions.

Once the core process works consistently, the business can consider additional channels, reports and integrations.

A connected DMC workflow is successful when every team can see the current position of the file and understand its own next action.
