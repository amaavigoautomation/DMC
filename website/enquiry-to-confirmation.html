From Enquiry to Confirmation: Where DMCs Lose Time—and How to Fix It

Faster confirmations begin with better handovers between sales, contracting and reservations.

An enquiry arrives with travel dates, an approximate budget and a list of destinations. Your sales team starts preparing the proposal. Contracting checks rates, someone follows up with a hotel, and another colleague searches for the latest transfer prices.

Then the itinerary changes.

A different hotel, an additional night or a revised passenger count sends the quotation back through the same process.

For many Destination Management Companies, the delay is spread across dozens of small tasks: finding information, clarifying requirements, repeating calculations and checking which version is current.

Improving enquiry-to-confirmation time starts with identifying those points of friction.

Capture the Information Needed to Build a Useful Quote

An enquiry can reach your team through email, a travel agent, a website or a sales representative. The format may differ, but your team still needs a consistent foundation.

Travel dates, passenger details, room requirements, service expectations, source market and budget all influence the quotation.

When those details are missing, consultants either make assumptions or pause to request clarification. Both can create additional work.

A structured enquiry record helps teams identify missing information early and assign clear ownership. It also makes the next follow-up easier when another colleague needs to assist.

Make Contracted Rates Accessible to Sales

A negotiated hotel rate has limited operational value if the sales team cannot find it or understand when it applies.

Rates may sit in spreadsheets, supplier emails or individual folders. Offers and cancellation conditions may be stored elsewhere.

The result is repeated checking before a consultant can confidently quote.

Organising contracts by product, validity, market and relevant conditions gives sales a clearer starting point. Contracting teams can focus on exceptions and negotiations instead of repeatedly answering the same rate questions.

Control Quotation Revisions

Revisions are a normal part of selling travel. The difficulty is keeping the commercial picture clear as the itinerary evolves.

Changing a hotel can affect accommodation cost, transfers, cancellation conditions and the overall margin. Adding a service can introduce another supplier and payment obligation.

A controlled quotation process should make it clear which option the agent is considering, what changed and which version was accepted.

This reduces the risk of confirming an earlier proposal or carrying an outdated service into the booking.

Give Reservations a Complete Handover

“Please book as quoted” is only useful when the accepted quotation contains everything reservations needs.

The handover should identify confirmed services, passenger requirements, agreed selling terms and any outstanding conditions.

Reservations also needs a clear way to distinguish between requested, pending, confirmed and amended services.

When status remains inside individual inboxes, sales may struggle to answer a simple question: “Is the booking confirmed?”

Shared visibility helps teams follow up on the actual pending items rather than chase the entire file.

Measure Where Work Waits

Speed should be measured at meaningful stages:

  • Time from enquiry receipt to first response.
  • Time from complete requirements to first quotation.
  • Time spent waiting for rate clarification.
  • Number of quotation revisions.
  • Time between customer acceptance and supplier confirmation.

These measures help management distinguish internal delays from supplier dependencies or customer changes.

How DMC24 Supports the Journey

The purpose is to let information move forward with the enquiry and booking, reducing the need to reconstruct it at each departmental handover.

A faster confirmation process begins when every team knows what has been agreed, what remains pending and what needs to happen next.
