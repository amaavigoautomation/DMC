Your Booking Revenue Looks Good. But Is Every File Profitable?

Understand what each booking earns after its actual service costs are accounted for.

A busy booking calendar can make a DMC feel successful. Sales are growing, suppliers are receiving reservations and guests are travelling.

But revenue alone cannot tell you whether those bookings are delivering the profit you expected.

A file that looked attractive at quotation stage may produce a smaller margin after amendments, supplier changes, additional services or unrecorded expenses.

For DMC owners and finance teams, file-wise profitability connects the sale to its financial outcome.

Expected Profit Is a Starting Point

At quotation stage, the business works with expected selling values and service costs.

Consider a simplified example:

ItemAt quotationAfter operation
Selling valueUSD 10,000USD 10,000
Direct service costsUSD 8,000USD 8,700
Gross profitUSD 2,000USD 1,300
Gross margin20%13%

This illustration excludes taxes and company overheads.

The booking still generates the same revenue. However, an additional USD 700 in direct costs reduces gross profit by 35%.

The important management question is why the cost changed—and whether the difference could have been anticipated, recovered or avoided.

Where Margin Can Reduce

Profit can change through ordinary operational decisions:

  • A hotel is replaced at a higher rate.
  • A guest receives an additional transfer.
  • A vehicle operates beyond the agreed service scope.
  • Supplier charges change after an amendment.
  • An expense is recorded after the quotation has been accepted.
  • A discount is offered without reviewing the resulting margin.

Some changes are justified and necessary. The issue is whether their financial effect remains visible.

Recording amendments and expenses against the relevant travel file helps the business understand the full cost of delivering that booking.

Review Profitability Throughout the Booking

Waiting until every supplier invoice arrives can leave management with information that is too late to influence the outcome.

A useful review process follows the file through three stages.

At quotation: Check the expected selling value, service costs and margin before offering the proposal.

During operation: Review amendments, additional services and changes to expected costs.

At closure: Reconcile supplier charges, recorded expenses and final selling values to establish the booking’s actual result.

This creates a clearer connection between commercial decisions and operational consequences.

Keep Profit and Collections Separate

A profitable booking can still create cash pressure if the customer has not paid.

Equally, a fully collected booking may have a weak margin once all supplier obligations are considered.

Management therefore needs to review profitability alongside receivables and payables. These are connected views of the business, but they answer different questions.

Profitability asks what the booking earned. Collection tracking asks what has been received and what remains outstanding.

Use File Results to Improve Future Sales

The value of file-wise P&L extends beyond identifying a disappointing booking.

Over time, it can help teams understand which products repeatedly require extra services, which quotations underestimate transport requirements and which commercial arrangements leave insufficient room for operational changes.

Those findings can inform future costing, supplier negotiations and pricing decisions.

Comparisons should account for differences in booking size, service mix and complexity rather than judging performance by revenue alone.
