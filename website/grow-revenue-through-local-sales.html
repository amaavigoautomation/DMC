Beyond the Original Booking: Grow Revenue Through Local Sales

Give representatives and destination teams a structured way to sell useful additional services after arrival.

The original booking rarely covers everything a traveller might want to experience.

After arrival, a guest may decide to visit an attraction, arrange a private transfer, extend a sightseeing programme or book an excursion for a free day.

Your local representatives, hotel desks and destination teams are often well placed to help.

The opportunity becomes more valuable when those sales are recorded, paid for and handed over to operations through a clear process.

Start with the Guest’s Actual Needs

Relevant recommendations begin with understanding the traveller’s existing programme.

Where are they staying? Which services have they already booked? How much time remains before departure? Are there practical requirements that affect the recommendation?

This context helps representatives suggest suitable additions and avoid duplicating services or creating schedule conflicts.

The objective is to offer something useful, with a clear explanation of what it includes and what it costs.

Give Sellers Approved Products and Prices

A representative should not need to contact the office for every routine product enquiry.

Access to an approved range of excursions, transfers, activities and tickets can make local selling more efficient.

However, the seller also needs the conditions that affect the guest’s decision: service dates, operating arrangements, inclusions, restrictions and cancellation terms.

Clear pricing and product information help maintain consistency across hotel desks, representatives and other selling points.

Bring Multiple Services into One Transaction

A guest requesting an airport transfer may also want an attraction ticket or a private city tour.

A cart-based process allows the seller to combine relevant services into a single transaction while keeping each component identifiable.

The guest should still be able to understand the individual services, total price and applicable conditions.

This creates a convenient purchasing experience without losing the details operations needs for fulfilment.

Record Collections Where the Sale Happens

Local sales can involve cash, card or configured digital payment methods, sometimes across more than one currency.

Each collection needs to be connected to the correct transaction and seller.

The business should be able to identify what was sold, what was collected, which payment method was used and whether anything remains outstanding.

Where representatives handle cash, a defined reconciliation and handover process remains essential alongside the software record.

Make Commissions Traceable

Commission arrangements can motivate a destination sales network, but the rules need to be clear.

Representatives should understand which services qualify, how commission is calculated and when it becomes payable.

The business also needs a consistent approach to amendments, cancellations and refunds that may affect the original commission.

Connecting commission records to the underlying sales helps teams review payouts and resolve questions with a clearer transaction history.

Connect Every Sale to Operations

A completed payment does not mean a service is ready to operate.

The relevant team may still need to arrange a supplier, assign transport, confirm a pickup time or issue documentation.

A useful local-sales workflow carries the service details into the wider booking and operational process, with a clear status and responsible team.

This helps prevent additional sales from becoming last-minute instructions that are difficult to track.

Measure the Channel’s Contribution

Reviewing local sales by representative, product, location and period can reveal where the channel is working well.

Revenue is only part of that assessment. Supplier costs, commissions, refunds and collection status also influence the result.

A high-selling location may require a different product mix or operating arrangement from another. Use the data to improve the channel rather than applying the same approach everywhere.
