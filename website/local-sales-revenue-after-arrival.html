How Local Sales Can Extend Revenue After Arrival

Turning representatives, hotel desks and destination counters into measurable sales channels.

Guests often make additional decisions once they reach a destination. A free afternoon becomes an opportunity for a private tour. A family decides to add an attraction. A traveller asks for a more convenient departure transfer.

For a DMC, these requests can become a meaningful sales channel when the business gives them clear ownership, suitable products and dependable fulfilment.

Building that channel starts with deciding where your team can help guests most effectively.

Choose the selling points deliberately

Hotel desks, local representatives, airport teams and destination offices have different opportunities to speak with travellers.

A guest arriving after a long flight may need practical assistance first. A representative meeting guests at their hotel may have more time to explain an excursion. The product range and approach should suit the setting.

Begin with locations where your team already has an appropriate guest relationship and the operational capacity to deliver the services offered.

Build a focused product range

A manageable range of clearly described services is easier to train and operate than an extensive catalogue introduced all at once.

Select products with understood operating conditions, dependable suppliers and transparent costs. Make sure sellers can explain the experience, inclusions, timing and relevant restrictions.

The range should serve actual guest needs and fit the available time in their programme. Unsuitable recommendations create operational problems and weaken trust.

Establish the economics of each sale

Local-sales revenue needs to be viewed alongside supplier cost, representative commission and any relevant collection or transaction costs.

Agree how discounts are handled and which decisions need approval. A sale can appear attractive at the counter while producing little contribution after its obligations are considered.

Use consistent records so that management can compare products and locations on a meaningful basis.

Connect selling capacity with delivery capacity

Selling an additional service creates a delivery commitment. Before expanding a channel, check whether reservations, suppliers and operations can support the likely demand.

Teams need clarity on service availability, booking cutoffs and how urgent requests are handled. A representative should know when a service needs further confirmation before making a promise to the guest.

This keeps sales ambitions aligned with what the destination operation can deliver.

Run a controlled pilot

A practical starting point is a limited product range through selected sellers or locations. Review both guest experience and operating effort during the pilot.

  • Which products are requested and purchased?
  • Which selling points generate suitable opportunities?
  • Are collections recorded and reconciled?
  • Do amendments or refunds reveal unclear product information?
  • Can operations fulfil the additional services consistently?

Use the findings to refine training, products and commercial rules before extending the model.
